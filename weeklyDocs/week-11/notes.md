# 🧠 Week 11 — Network Actively

**AI Prompt Engineer Journey · Khaled**

> **Goal this week:** Most jobs come from conversations, not cold applications. This week you build the conversation layer on top of your application layer.

---

## The Right Framing for Outreach

Cold outreach works when it's genuinely curious and genuinely specific — not templated. The goal of a first message isn't to ask for a job. It's to ask one specific question that only someone in that role can answer.

**What works:**
> "I'm building towards a prompt engineer role and noticed you've been working on LLM evaluation at [Company]. Would you have 15 minutes to share how your team approaches testing prompts at scale? I've built my own eval framework as part of a personal project and I'm curious how it differs from production approaches."

**What doesn't work:**
> "Hi, I'm interested in AI and would love to connect and learn more about what you do."

The first is a person asking a question. The second is a person looking for something without offering anything specific in return.

## What to Do This Week

**Follow people in the field.** Following is low-commitment and puts their content in your feed. Start here.

**Comment with genuine substance.** Five substantive comments on posts from people in the AI field this week. Specific, adds something, doesn't just agree. This is more valuable than most cold DMs.

**Send 3–5 direct messages.** Use the framing above. Not asking for a job — asking one specific question you'd genuinely value the answer to.

**Apply alongside networking.** Continue submitting applications. Networking multiplies applications — it doesn't replace them.

---

## What to Do If Someone Responds

If someone responds to a DM, reply quickly. If they offer a call, accept it. Prepare 3 genuine questions you want answers to. Take notes. Follow up with a short thank-you message that references something specific they said.

The goal of a networking call isn't to ask "are you hiring?" — it's to learn something useful. The byproduct of learning from people in the field is that they remember you when something comes up.
