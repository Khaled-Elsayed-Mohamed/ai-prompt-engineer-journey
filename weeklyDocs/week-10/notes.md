## Application Strategy

### The 5-Minute Cover Note

Not a cover letter. A note. Three sentences:

1. What caught your eye about this role (specific)
2. The most relevant project you've built (one sentence, link to GitHub)
3. What you'd bring (one sentence connecting your niche to their context)

Example:
> "I'm interested in the AI Engineer role because [Company]'s focus on document automation aligns with the niche I've been building in. My most relevant project is a meeting notes processor that extracts structured decisions and action items from raw notes — live on GitHub with a full test suite. I'd bring prompt engineering experience focused on internal business tools and a habit of documenting everything I build."

That's it. Under 80 words. More likely to be read than a 4-paragraph letter.

### Targeting the Application

Before applying to any role:
- Read the job description twice
- Identify the one word in your note that should be different for this specific company
- Check LinkedIn to see if you have a second-degree connection

## What to Post on LinkedIn This Week

One post — something genuinely useful you learned while building your projects. Not a job announcement. Not "excited to share." A short insight:

> "Something I learned building a document Q&A system: the hardest behavior to prompt-engineer is 'I don't know.' Models want to help — they'll fill gaps with plausible-sounding information if you don't explicitly instruct them to say 'the answer isn't in this document.' The fix is a combination of: naming the failure mode explicitly in the prompt, and a second validation prompt that checks whether the answer is supported by the source text."

That kind of post builds visibility with people who hire for exactly this.
